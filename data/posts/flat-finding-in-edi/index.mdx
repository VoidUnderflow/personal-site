# Preliminaries

Edinburgh is a pretty nice place to visit, and sometimes even live in. But renting here has had its ... challenges.
In the first two sections, I'll try to give some practical advice based on my own past experience and the last section relays the funnier bits of said past experience.

# What am I looking for in a flat?

I've been living in Edinburgh on-and-off for around 7 years, starting when I was a student. I've learned what I like, what I dislike what I can tolerate and what I won't. Obviously, when you're a student with no income from a middle class Romanian family - your choices are limited. Said choices open up quite a bit if you have the money.

My current criteria:

1. No mice; I've lived in a mice-infested flat before and it's not fun.

2. Reasonably warm in the winter or at the very least not impossible to heat without the electricity/gas bill bankrupting you. What's warm for me is ~20[deg symbol?] degrees. What's warm for a Scottish person seems to be ~10, and that's probably considered toasty.

3. Max one other flatmate. If you're a student you can't really be picky about this unfortunately. I kinda prefer things being calm and quiet, the more people there are the more sh\*t you have to deal with. Unexpected guests (I remember the time 5 friends of my flatmate's boyfriend were in my kitchen when I got home, doing pre-drinks; too old for this stuff now).

4. No weird smells.

# Tips

1. No visible cracks between the floor and the wall (see "critterion" #1).

2. Being on the ground floor can be kinda sketchy. Always felt weird about people on the street being able to see me inside my room. Ground floor flats are also harder to heat, and more prone to having mice. Basement-level flats are included + they can have weird smells. Generally, the higher the flat is the warmer it'll be. With the summers getting hotter though, too high up can mean temps like 32 degrees (happened this summer - currently renting an attic).

3. Finding a student flat is much easier around winter. It's awful, but a lot of people seemed to drop out or take a break because the exams were too stressful etc. Lots of spare rooms all around.

4. Private renting in July-August-September is uhh impossible. Every landlord with an empty room has two choices: rent their room for anywhere between 2x-5x the price for a month or two to Fringe-people or rent it for a sucker. It's a no-brainer. So yeah, pretty much everything's taken. September's when students arrive so prices are pretty competitive then too. [TODO: after all, why shouldn't I (Bilbo.jpg)]

5. Always look at the state of the windows. Any draft coming through? Are they older than Thatcher? Probably skip, although this is manageable. A thick curtain and some light DIY and you can make it serviceable.

6. I also always look at the state of the washing machine. Look under the rubbery bit in the front of the drum. If it's absolutely disgusting, I'd be a lot more careful with hidden issues. This is more of a vibe check than anything - it's such a PITA to clean (had the displeasure of having to do it twice, in two different flats).

7. If renting through an agency, check reviews (not the ones on their websites, obv) and forums. There are a couple that should be avoided at all costs, and it's not hard to find out which. Not all of them are bad - but most of them are.

8. Always view in person. Don't assume that it's going to be ok if it's pricier. Most agencies require an in-person viewing and for good reason. I'm more of a risk taker so I skipped this by using private landlords. 33% chance of success so far.

9. The closer you are to the center, the higher the discrepancy between a low-rent and a high-rent flat will be. If it's cheap and in the center, it's going to be absolutely shit. The only exception is with private landlords, specifically people in their 30s buying their first flat and just wanting some help with their mortgage. You can get some pretty good deals this way, but they go fast and are a few and far between.

## Gentlemen, a short view back to the past

### Y1

It all started in 2015, as a University of Edinburgh freshman. When I got out of the Airlink and asked the driver for directions, I shockingly realised that I can't understand Scottish accents. Who could have expected that, given that I learned English by watching American TV shows?

First year, the uni lets you stay in one of their accommodations. Most of them are pretty nice. Catered vs un-catered was when my class self-awareness was born. My first year accommodation was Fraser Court, which was close enough to Pollock Halls to have dinner there sometime and be a bit envious of all the rich kids. I was extremely culinary challenged, the lowest point in my first year was making a pizza using a Sainsbury's pre-made dough, pasta sauce, olives, and ... fried eggs. Flat was nice, flatmates were nice enough - nothing to complain.

But the Uni lures you into a false sense of security, since at the end of your first year they just boot you in the deep end of Edinburgh's rental market. Reminds me of when my father tried to teach me how to swim and I almost drowned.

I made some friends, so we started looking for a flat as a group of 3, which then grew to 4 then to 5, then the last peep found a flat, then two others found a flat together so I was left with a friend, super close to our flights back home in May, without a flat for next year. ### Y3 part 1 - the return to FC

After such an experience, I was excited to return to uni accommodation by any means necessary. So I did what every other _cough_ budget Eastern European student did and became an RA. You pay like 25% of the rent, but have a sort-of-permanent part-time job. I was secretly hoping for a spot in a catered accommodation - not having to worry about eating would have been a huge QOL.

Good on paper, horrible in practice. I liked organising events, my sister helped me with some of the posters which was more than my coworkers/flatmates ever did. We just didn't get along, made a few initial mistakes which snowballed into getting excluded from event planning. Having to plan events alone, while Uni was getting harder, my summer project extending well into November, meant that I just gave up after a semester. Living with coworkers is never a good idea, hope I won't have to do that one again.

Hmm alternative (merge into something better?):
Lasted for a semester. I had other RA friends and they and their colleagues were helping each other, organising events together, etc. Mine were… less generous. Added stress, uni stress, having to finish a website for a summer project while doing uni work led to me being very burnt out and just ditching the bit that I disliked the most. Having coworkers as flatmates - never doing that one again. If any of them are reading this - I apologise for not being a good flatmate - but you weren’t either. I remember the event planning sessions I was excluded from, wasn’t very nice.

### Y3 part 2

Finding a flat in January was surprisingly easy. The guy I replaced had finished his PhD and was taking a much needed break as a Research Assistant someplace else.

People in the flat were amazing, super friendly and the price was alright. Bonus points is that it was just 5 mins away from where I was staying. Leaving that place, with my friends helping me move out in the middle of the night, is a very fond and warm memory. Felt like the ending of Disco Elysium, when the folk from the precinct escort you out as a hero. Battered, bruised, but the future looks bright-er. As well as my flatmate who had such an amazing voice - the only shower singing I didn't mind (is this weird?). She went on to work for a bank, although I reckon she'd be the kind of person who could randomly win a The Voice if that thing's still going on (don't have a TV license).

The flat itself though, ground floor. That winter was very snowy, so it was cold as shit. Also, front door had a bit of a gap and my room was the first on the left. This meant that mice were pretty fond of my room. Their excitement was apparent in all the fun they had under my bed. Those noises left my psyche scarred and deformed. Whenever someone says that mice are cute, I nod politely, tag them as an absolutely insane person and move on with my life.

Edinburgh is a pretty nice place to visit, and sometimes even live in. But renting here has had its ... challenges.

This is a two parter. Here's where I provide a little context and [here's](/posts/renting-edi-advice) where I give some hopefully slightly-more-than-common sense advice.

## Gentlemen, a short view back to the past

### Y1

It all started in 2015, as a University of Edinburgh freshman. When I got out of the Airlink and asked the driver for directions, I shockingly realised that I can't understand Scottish. Who could have expected that, given that I learned English by watching American TV shows? Choosing to take TOEFL over IELTS might have been a bit of a hint now that I look back.

First year, the uni lets you stay in one of their accommodations for a pretty reasonable price given the market. Most of them are pretty nice. Catered vs un-catered was when my class self-awareness was born. My first year accommodation was un-catered, but close enough to Pollock Halls to have dinner there sometime and be a bit envious of all the rich kids. I was extremely culinary challenged. The lowest point in my first year was making a pizza using a Sainsbury's pre-made dough, pasta sauce, olives, and ... fried eggs.

The flat was nice and warm, the flatmates were alright so I couldn't really complain about anything. It was a nice enough time.

But the Uni lures you into a false sense of security, since at the end of your first year they just boot you in the deep end of Edinburgh's rental market. Reminded me of my first attempt at learning how to swim. Both had the same result.

Towards the end of my first year, I started looking for a flat with one of my friends. Our two-person group quickly grew to 3, then to 4, 5 then shrunk to 4, then back to me and my friend. I'll spare you the associated drama. The end result is that we lost a lot of time and we found ourselves without a flat for next year, in mid-May, really close to our flights home.

I was thinking of giving up and switching to a uni back home, but a friend of a friend was looking for two flatmates for the low low price of 320 pounds per month (in 2016). What a boon! We rushed to a viewing, promptly ignored all the red flags, signed a contract and went back home fairly content.

### Y2

That was the worst flat I've ever lived in and I vowed to never be put in a position to live like that again.

It had the worst mice infestation I've ever seen. It was honestly remarkable given that it was a 4th floor flat. I routinely found droppings in my shoes, in the bathroom, in the dish rack. I'm sure I can still find some of them in my psyche.

The mice were some sort of mutants that developed a taste for eating poisoned grains. The only thing those achieved was color their droppings blue. The washing machine was much more efficient at killing mice, at the cost of us throwing away the affected clothes. I'll spare you the photos this time.

The fridge was broken all the time. Our landlord was un-contactable, which led me to be on the contract for one extra year after I left that place even though I stopped paying. Hell, if someone looks hard enough, I might still figure as a tenant there.

My room had four holes through the concrete wall. I still don't understand what those were about. A Google search said this was for ventilation? Jury's still out, but what's certain is that they made winter fun. I plugged them as best as I could, but ended up having to put my bed next to the heater and sleeping with it on. Finding the optimal distance between the bed and the heater took a few iterations, but my burnt elbows were an acceptable cost.

My friend's room had a strong and persistent mold smell that made it really hard to breathe. We soon found out why. We all know that correlation does not mean causation, but every heavy rain outside seem to be accompanied by a lighter rain inside.

### Y3 part 1 - the return to FC

After gaining so much life experience in such a short amount of time, I was excited to return to uni accommodation. The by-any-means-necessary kind of excited. So I did what every other _cough_ economically-challenged Eastern European student did and became an RA. The math was simple. You paid like a fraction of the rent, at the cost of having a sort-of-permanent part-time job. I was dreaming of getting a spot in catered accommodation, but alas, it was not to be.

It seemed like such a good idea on paper, but ended up being horrible in practice. I was fooled by all of my RA friends into believing that it was an easy job, RAs had each others' backs and that wardens were people who understood that this job was something students were doing for survival and that their studies still took priority. I never really got along with my team. Part of it was of course my fault. I made a few initial mistakes (it was basically my first job), uni was getting harder, my summer project extended well into November and I ended up being excluded from the informal team event planning meetings. My grades started to suffer and all the extra pressure led to other issues down the line.

My coworkers could have easily chosen to be a bit more charitable with their intepretations of me, but alas no slack was cut. The bitterness of these five months faded in time, but I still wouldn't say hi to all but one of my coworkers if I randomly encountered them today.

### Y3 part 2

No longer being an RA meant that I had to once again find some place to rent. In January, this turned out to be surprisingly easy. Some guy was finishing his PhD and wanted to take a much needed break as a Research Assistant someplace else.

Bonus points is that it was just 5 mins away from where I was staying, so moving out was quick. Leaving that place with my friends helping me move out in the middle of the night, is a very fond and warm memory. It felt a bit like the good ending of Disco Elysium.

People in the flat were amazing, super friendly and the price was alright. One of my flatmates was the kind of person who'd randomly go on and win a singing show. The only shower overture I didn't mind.

The flat itself though was ground floor. That winter was very snowy, so it was cold as shit. The front door had a bit of a gap and my room was next to it. This meant that mice were pretty fond of my room, and the warmth it offered. Their excitement for it was apparent given all the fun they were having under my bed. I can still remember the noise they were making scurrying about.

Whenever someone says that mice are cute, I nod politely, tag them as an absolutely mental person and move on with my life.

### Y4 and beyond

The uni had a hidden treasure trove of private flats they owned. Every year they hold a raffle for them and every year so far I'd lost. Renting privately through an agency or whatever was still too expensive.

Luck smiled upon me though and a friend was moving out of one of them. With a semi-legal move, me and my Y2 flatmate managed to get that one for ourselves. Was it ideal? For a student it was. A Tesco below us, Appleton Tower 5 mins away, what more could you want?! You take the good with the bad. The used needles, frequent peeing on the front door, and random drug-fueled screams in the middle of the night were honestly an acceptable cost.

# What this website will and won't be

First of all, I want the Projects page to be a portfolio of a kind. So far, what I've done has limited usefulness -- mostly to learn new languages / frameworks / tools. Maybe that will change, maybe it won't.

I'm not a fan of social media, LinkedIn in particular. Can't quite put my finger on why, there's just something dystopian and eerie about it. People who take themselves too seriously, suits, everything being public -- being there is uncomfortable, and not the good kind. On this website, I'll put things I'd put on LinkedIn anyway, but I have more control over it and it's much less overwhelming.

My posts will probably be a mix of tech and personal stuff (books I've read, thoughts I've had, things I've been through). I think that it's a bit sad that a lot of people in tech lose their connection to the humanities or look down on them. Maybe that's why we are where we are right now. I will keep posts somewhat professional though and will (hopefully) avoid controversial topics (e.g: politics, religion, etc.). That being said, I like being honest and open so some personal posts may broach difficult topics and have a sadder tone and that's fine. Life isn't only sunshine and rainbows, after all.

# LLM usage

One positive thing about LLMs is that they taught me about the existence of the em-dash -- and that there's no such thing of using too many.

None of the posts here will be written by an LLM, as I feel like it would defeat the purpose.

I'm honestly somewhere in the middle when it comes to AI -- I don't buy the "it will replace everyone" shtick and the overwhelming amount of hype, but they can be pretty useful if used responsibly. A general rule of thumb I have is that thinking generally remains on-prem.

I tried vibe coding some projects and found that it pretty much takes away all the fun, and it's also unclear whether it actually saves time. LLM usage also blurs my mental map of the code I'm working with, thus making me even more reliant on them (🚩#1).

I'm also pretty wary of external dependencies in general, and nothing stops the big AI companies from making their models more expensive whenever they feel like it, or pulling them back altogether (🚩#2).

# Why VoidUnderflow?

We know what an integer underflow is, but how about a void underflow? The name is something that has meaning to me and also sounds sufficiently tech-y that someone discovering it won't think too much about it.

Its origin is a mantra of a character from the Avatar series (not the one with the blue guys), which stuck with me.

It's a state of mind I sometimes reach after mindfulness meditation, a feeling of openness to the world and a personal answer to nihilism.
